# 📖 باب ششم: المؤنث وأسماء الإشارة للمؤنث (هٰذِهِ اور تِلْكَ)
# Chapter 6: Feminine Nouns & Feminine Pointers (Haadhihi & Tilka)

> **Master Tutor Law #6 (The Feminine Markers)**: 
> *In Arabic, every noun is born masculine by default, unless it carries the crown of femininity: the Taa Marbutah (**ـة / ة**), is female by biology (**أُمّ / بِنْت**), or belongs to the sacred club of paired body parts (**عَيْن / يَد / رِجْل / أُذُن**).*

---

## 👨‍🏫 Part 1: Tutor Narrative & Conceptual Awakening

In English, objects have no gender: a pen is "it", a car is "it", and a chair is "it". 
In Urdu, everything has gender: "قلم گر گیا" (masculine), "گاڑی چل پڑی" (feminine).

Arabic also assigns gender to every noun! But unlike Urdu or French where you often have to memorize arbitrary genders, **Arabic gives you clear, visual signs for 95% of feminine words**.

### How do you spot a Feminine Word in Arabic?
1. **The Taa Marbutah (التاء المربوطة — `ـة / ة`)**:
   This is the round "tied" Taa. Any noun ending in this letter is treated as feminine:
   - **سَيَّارَةٌ** (*Sayyaaratun* — a car)
   - **سَاعَةٌ** (*Saa'atun* — a watch)
   - **مَدْرَسَةٌ** (*Madrasatun* — a school)
2. **Natural Female Beings (المؤنث الحقيقي)**:
   Even if they lack a Taa Marbutah, mothers, sisters, and daughters are feminine:
   - **أُمٌّ** (*Ummun* — mother)
   - **بِنْتٌ** (*Bintun* — girl / daughter)
   - **أُخْتٌ** (*Ukhtun* — sister)
3. **Paired Body Parts (أعضاء الجسد المزدوجة)**:
   Body parts that come in pairs (two eyes, two ears, two hands, two feet) are classically feminine:
   - **عَيْنٌ** (*'Aynun* — eye)
   - **أُذُنٌ** (*Udhunun* — ear)
   - **يَدٌ** (*Yadun* — hand)
   - **رِجْلٌ** (*Rijlun* — leg / foot)
   *(Single body parts like أَنْفٌ [nose] and رَأْسٌ [head] are masculine).*

## 🎯 Introducing the Feminine Demonstratives: هٰذِهِ & تِلْكَ

When pointing to a feminine noun, you cannot use **هٰذَا** or **ذٰلِكَ**! Arabic has dedicated royal feminine pointers:

| Distance | Masculine Pointer | Feminine Pointer | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Near (قریب)** | **هٰذَا** (*Haadhaa*) | **هٰذِهِ** (*Haadhihi*) | This is |
| **Distant (دور)** | **ذٰلِكَ** (*Dhaalika*) | **تِلْكَ** (*Tilka*) | That is |

- **هٰذَا وَلَدٌ** (*This is a boy*) $\longleftrightarrow$ **هٰذِهِ بِنْتٌ** (*This is a girl*).
- **ذٰلِكَ مَسْجِدٌ** (*That is a mosque*) $\longleftrightarrow$ **تِلْكَ مَدْرَسَةٌ** (*That is a school*).

## 📐 Part 3: Visual Grammar Formulas & Blueprints (قواعد)

### Blueprint 1: Near Feminine Sentence Blueprint
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{هٰذِهِ}} + [\text{اسْمٌ مُؤَنَّثٌ}\text{ (Ends in ٌ)}] = \text{جُمْلَةٌ (This is a [fem] ...)}}$$

```
   [ هٰذِهِ ]            +         [ سَيَّارَةٌ ]        =      هٰذِهِ سَيَّارَةٌ
 (Feminine Pointer)     +      (Feminine Noun)      =    "This is a car."
```

### Blueprint 2: Distant Feminine Sentence Blueprint
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{تِلْكَ}} + [\text{اسْمٌ مُؤَنَّثٌ}\text{ (Ends in ٌ)}] = \text{جُمْلَةٌ (That is a [fem] ...)}}$$

```
   [ تِلْكَ ]            +         [ بَقَرَةٌ ]         =      تِلْكَ بَقَرَةٌ
 (Distant Fem. Pointer) +      (Feminine Noun)      =    "That is a cow."
```

### Blueprint 3: Converting Masculine Adjectives to Feminine
To describe a female person or feminine object, simply glue a **Taa Marbutah (`ـة`)** to the masculine word:
- مُدَرِّسٌ (*male teacher*) $\xrightarrow{\,+\,\text{ة}\,}$ **مُدَرِّسَةٌ** (*female teacher*)
- طَالِبٌ (*male student*) $\xrightarrow{\,+\,\text{ة}\,}$ **طَالِبَةٌ** (*female student*)
- طَبِيبٌ (*male doctor*) $\xrightarrow{\,+\,\text{ة}\,}$ **طَبِيبَةٌ** (*female doctor*)
- جَدِيدٌ (*new [m]*) $\xrightarrow{\,+\,\text{ة}\,}$ **جَدِيدَةٌ** (*new [f]*)

## 💬 Part 4: Reading Sentences & Dialogue Studio (قراءة ومحادثة)

Read these 12 sentences, contrasting masculine and feminine forms:

1. **هٰذَا ابْنُ حَامِدٍ وَهٰذِهِ بِنْتُ يَاسِرٍ.**  
   *Haadhaa ibnu Haamidin wa haadhihi bintu Yaasirin.*  
   Urdu: یہ حامد کا بیٹا ہے اور یہ یاسر کی بیٹی ہے۔  
   English: This is Hamid's son and this is Yasir's daughter.

2. **ابْنُ حَامِدٍ جَالِسٌ وَبِنْتُ يَاسِرٍ وَاقِفَةٌ.**  
   *Ibnu Haamidin jaalisun wa bintu Yaasirin waaqifatun.*  
   Urdu: حامد کا بیٹا بیٹھا ہے اور یاسر کی بیٹی کھڑی ہے۔  
   English: Hamid's son is sitting and Yasir's daughter is standing.

3. **مَنْ هٰذِهِ؟ هٰذِهِ أُخْتُ الْمُهَنْدِسِ.**  
   *Man haadhihi? Haadhihi ukhtu al-muhandisi.*  
   Urdu: یہ کون ہیں؟ یہ انجینئر صاحب کی بہن ہیں۔  
   English: Who is this? This is the engineer's sister.

4. **أَهِيَ أَيْضاً مُهَنْدِسَةٌ؟ لَا، هِيَ طَبِيبَةٌ.**  
   *A-hiya aydan muhandisatun? Laa, hiya tabeebatun.*  
   Urdu: کیا وہ بھی انجینئر ہیں؟ نہیں، وہ ڈاکٹر ہیں۔  
   English: Is she also an engineer? No, she is a doctor.

5. **سَيَّارَةُ مَنْ هٰذِهِ؟ هٰذِهِ سَيَّارَةُ الْمُدِيرِ.**  
   *Sayyaaratu man haadhihi? Haadhihi sayyaaratu al-mudiri.*  
   Urdu: یہ کس کی کار ہے؟ یہ مدیر (پرنسپل) کی کار ہے۔  
   English: Whose car is this? This is the director's car.

6. **مَا هٰذِهِ؟ هٰذِهِ مِكْوَاةٌ.**  
   *Maa haadhihi? Haadhihi mikwaatun.*  
   Urdu: یہ کیا ہے؟ یہ ایک استری ہے۔  
   English: What is this? This is an iron.

7. **لِمَنْ هٰذِهِ؟ هٰذِهِ لِخَالِدٍ.**  
   *Liman haadhihi? Haadhihi li-Khaalidin.*  
   Urdu: یہ کس کی ہے؟ یہ خالد کی ہے۔  
   English: Whose is this? This belongs to Khalid.

8. **أَدَرَّاجَةُ أَنَسٍ هٰذِهِ؟ لَا، هٰذِهِ دَرَّاجَةُ عَمَّارٍ.**  
   *A-darraajatu Anasin haadhihi? Laa, haadhihi darraajatu 'Ammaarin.*  
   Urdu: کیا یہ انس کی سائیکل ہے؟ نہیں، یہ عمار کی سائیکل ہے۔  
   English: Is this Anas's bicycle? No, this is Ammar's bicycle.

9. **هٰذِهِ السَّاعَةُ جَمِيلَةٌ جِدّاً.**  
   *Haadhihi as-saa'atu jameelatun jiddan.*  
   Urdu: یہ گھڑی بہت خوبصورت ہے۔  
   English: This watch is very beautiful.

10. **هٰذِهِ مِلْعَقَةٌ فِي الْقِدْرِ، وَالْمِلْعَقَةُ فِي الْقِدْرِ.**  
    *Haadhihi mil'aqatun fee al-qidri.*  
    Urdu: یہ ہانڈی میں چمچ ہے۔  
    English: This is a spoon in the cooking pot.

11. **مَا تِلْكَ؟ تِلْكَ بَقَرَةٌ.**  
    *Maa tilka? Tilka baqaratun.*  
    Urdu: وہ کیا ہے؟ وہ ایک گائے ہے۔  
    English: What is that? That is a cow.

12. **هٰذِهِ عَيْنٌ، وَهٰذِهِ أُذُنٌ، وَهٰذِهِ يَدٌ، وَهٰذِهِ رِجْلٌ.**  
    *Haadhihi 'aynun, wa haadhihi udhunun, wa haadhihi yadun, wa haadhihi rijlun.*  
    Urdu: یہ آنکھ ہے، اور یہ کان ہے، اور یہ ہاتھ ہے، اور یہ پاؤں ہے۔  
    English: This is an eye, and this is an ear, and this is a hand, and this is a foot.

## ⚠️ Part 5: Common Beginner Traps & Master Tutor Pro-Tips

### Trap 1: Pointing at a car with هٰذَا
- **Mistake**: Saying `هٰذَا سَيَّارَةٌ`.
- **Master Rule**: Because **سَيَّارَةٌ** ends in Taa Marbutah (`ة`), it is strictly feminine! You must use **هٰذِهِ سَيَّارَةٌ**.

### Trap 2: Missing the femininity of body parts
- **Mistake**: Saying `هٰذَا عَيْنٌ` or `هٰذَا يَدٌ`.
- **Master Rule**: Body parts that you have two of (eyes, ears, hands, feet) are treated as feminine in classical Arabic. Always point to them with **هٰذِهِ**.

### Trap 3: Pronouncing the Taa Marbutah at the end of a sentence
- **Phonetics**: When pausing (*Waqf*) on a word ending in Taa Marbutah (`ـة`), the 'T' sound softens into a gentle 'H' sound:
  - *Sayyaaratun* becomes *Sayyaarah*.
  - *Madrasatun* becomes *Madrasah*.

#### Drill 1 Answers:
1. **هٰذَا** قَلَمٌ. (Pen is masculine).
2. **هٰذِهِ** سَيَّارَةٌ. (Car ends in Taa Marbutah $\to$ feminine).
3. **هٰذِهِ** بِنْتٌ. (Daughter is natural female $\to$ feminine).
4. **هٰذَا** مَسْجِدٌ. (Mosque is masculine).
5. **هٰذِهِ** عَيْنٌ. (Eye is a paired body part $\to$ feminine).
6. **هٰذِهِ** سَاعَةٌ. (Watch ends in Taa Marbutah $\to$ feminine).

#### Drill 2 Answers:
1. **هٰذِهِ طَالِبَةٌ جَدِيدَةٌ.** (Pointer becomes هٰذِهِ, noun and adjective take Taa Marbutah).
2. **الْبِنْتُ جَالِسَةٌ.** (Subject becomes feminine, predicate takes Taa Marbutah).
3. **تِلْكَ طَبِيبَةٌ.** (Distant masculine ذٰلِكَ becomes distant feminine تِلْكَ, and doctor takes Taa Marbutah).
