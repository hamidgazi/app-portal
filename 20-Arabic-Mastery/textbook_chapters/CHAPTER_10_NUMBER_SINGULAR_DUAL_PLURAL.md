# 📖 باب دہم: العدد — المفرد والمثنى والجمع (واحد، تثنیہ اور جمع)
# Chapter 10: Number in Arabic — Singular, Dual, & Plural (Mufrad, Muthanna, Jam')

> **Master Tutor Law #10 (The Non-Human Plural Rule)**: 
> *In Arabic, broken and non-human plurals (books, pens, mosques, cars) are legally treated as a **Single Lady** (Singular Feminine)! You point to them with **هٰذِهِ** and describe them with **جَدِيدَةٌ**.*

---

## 👨‍🏫 Part 1: Tutor Narrative & Conceptual Awakening

In English, grammar is binary: an object is either **Singular** (one) or **Plural** (more than one). 
In Arabic, however, mathematics and linguistics merge into a 3-tier structure:
1. **المُفْرَد (Al-Mufrad — Singular)**: Exactly 1.
2. **المُثَنَّى (Al-Muthannaa — Dual)**: Exactly 2.
3. **الْجَمْع (Al-Jam' — Plural)**: 3 or more.

### 1. The Dual (تثنیہ — Exactly Two):
To make any singular noun refer to two items, you don't add the number "two". You simply weld the royal dual suffix **ـَانِ** (*-aani*) to the end:
- كِتَابٌ (*one book*) $\longrightarrow$ **كِتَابَانِ** (*two books*)
- قَلَمٌ (*one pen*) $\longrightarrow$ **قَلَمَانِ** (*two pens*)
- بِنْتٌ (*one girl*) $\longrightarrow$ **بِنْتَانِ** (*two girls*)

---

### 2. The 3 Great Tribes of Plurals:
When counting 3 or more, Arabic branches into three distinct plural systems:

#### Tribe A: The Sound Masculine Plural (جَمْعُ الْمُذَكَّرِ السَّالِمُ)
Called "sound" (*saalim*) because the singular root word remains completely unharmed! You simply add **ـُونَ** (*-oona* in Marfoo') or **ـِينَ** (*-eena* in Majroor):
- مُسْلِمٌ $\longrightarrow$ **مُسْلِمُونَ** (*Muslims*)
- مُدَرِّسٌ $\longrightarrow$ **مُدَرِّسُونَ** (*Teachers*)
- مُجْتَهِدٌ $\longrightarrow$ **مُجْتَهِدُونَ** (*Hardworking people*)

#### Tribe B: The Sound Feminine Plural (جَمْعُ الْمُؤَنَّثِ السَّالِمُ)
Drop the singular Taa Marbutah and add the long feminine suffix **ـَاتٌ** (*-aatun*):
- مُسْلِمَةٌ $\longrightarrow$ **مُسْلِمَاتٌ** (*Muslim women*)
- سَيَّارَةٌ $\longrightarrow$ **سَيَّارَاتٌ** (*Cars*)
- سَاعَةٌ $\longrightarrow$ **سَاعَاتٌ** (*Watches / hours*)

#### Tribe C: The Broken Plural (جَمْعُ التَّكْسِيرِ)
Just like English "foot $\to$ feet" or "child $\to$ children", the internal vowel architecture of the singular word "breaks" and rearranges into classic rhythmic patterns:
- كِتَابٌ $\longrightarrow$ **كُتُبٌ** (*Books*)
- قَلَمٌ $\longrightarrow$ **أَقْلَامٌ** (*Pens*)
- رَجُلٌ $\longrightarrow$ **رِجَالٌ** (*Men*)
- بَيْتٌ $\longrightarrow$ **بُيُوتٌ** (*Houses*)
- مَسْجِدٌ $\longrightarrow$ **مَسَاجِدُ** (*Mosques*)

---

## 👑 The Supreme Secret: Non-Human Plurals = Singular Feminine!

Memorize this golden rule; it will unlock hundreds of verses in the Quran:
> **كُلُّ جَمْعٍ لِغَيْرِ الْعَاقِلِ يُعَامَلُ مُعَامَلَةَ الْمُفْرَدِ الْمُؤَنَّثِ**  
> *"Every plural of an inanimate object or animal is grammatically treated as a Singular Feminine noun."*

Look at what this means in practice:
- If you point to one pen: **هٰذَا قَلَمٌ** (*This is a pen* — Masc. Singular).
- If you point to ten pens: **هٰذِهِ أَقْلَامٌ** (*These are pens* — **هٰذِهِ** is Fem. Singular!).
- "The pens are new": **الْأَقْلَامُ جَدِيدَةٌ** (**جَدِيدَةٌ** is Fem. Singular!).

Only human beings with intellect (men, women, teachers, doctors) receive true plural treatment:
- **هٰؤُلَاءِ رِجَالٌ** (*These are men* — Human Plural Pointer).
- **أُولٰئِكَ طُلَّابٌ** (*Those are students* — Human Distant Plural Pointer).

## 📐 Part 3: Visual Grammar Formulas & Blueprints (قواعد)

### Blueprint 1: The Human Plural Pointer Formula
For human groups, use **هٰؤُلَاءِ** (*Haa'ulaa'i* — These) and **أُولٰئِكَ** (*Ulaa'ika* — Those):
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{هٰؤُلَاءِ}} + [\text{جَمْعُ عاقِلٍ}] = \text{هٰؤُلَاءِ طُلَّابٌ (These are students)}}$$
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{أُولٰئِكَ}} + [\text{جَمْعُ عاقِلٍ}] = \text{أُولٰئِكَ رِجَالٌ (Those are men)}}$$

### Blueprint 2: The Non-Human Plural Formula (The Feminine Law)
For non-human plurals, use the Singular Feminine pointer **هٰذِهِ** (*This*) and **تِلْكَ** (*That*):
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{هٰذِهِ}} + [\text{جَمْعُ غَيْرِ عَاقِلٍ}] = \mathbf{\text{هٰذِهِ كُتُبٌ}} \quad (\text{These are books})}$$
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{تِلْكَ}} + [\text{جَمْعُ غَيْرِ عَاقِلٍ}] = \mathbf{\text{تِلْكَ بُيُوتٌ}} \quad (\text{Those are houses})}$$

### Blueprint 3: Non-Human Adjective Agreement
$$\boxed{[\text{Non-Human Plural Subject}] + [\text{Singular Feminine Predicate}]}$$
$$\text{الْكُتُبُ} \quad + \quad \text{جَدِيدَةٌ} \quad = \quad \mathbf{\text{الْكُتُبُ جَدِيدَةٌ}} \quad (\text{The books are new.})$$
$$\text{الْمَسَاجِدُ} \quad + \quad \text{كَبِيرَةٌ} \quad = \quad \mathbf{\text{الْمَسَاجِدُ كَبِيرَةٌ}} \quad (\text{The mosques are large.})$$

## 💬 Part 4: Reading Sentences & Dialogue Studio (قراءة ومحادثة)

Read these 12 sentences, actively contrasting human plurals with non-human plurals:

1. **مَنْ هٰؤُلَاءِ الرِّجَالُ؟ هُمْ تُجَّارٌ مِنَ الْعِرَاقِ.**  
   *Man haa'ulaa'i ar-rijaalu? Hum tujjaarun mina al-'Iraaqi.*  
   Urdu: یہ مرد کون ہیں؟ وہ عراق سے تاجر ہیں۔  
   English: Who are these men? They are merchants from Iraq.

2. **هٰؤُلَاءِ طُلَّابٌ جُدُدٌ، وَأُولٰئِكَ طُلَّابٌ قُدَامَى.**  
   *Haa'ulaa'i tullaabun jududun, wa ulaa'ika tullaabun qudaamaa.*  
   Urdu: یہ نئے طلبہ ہیں اور وہ پرانے طلبہ ہیں۔  
   English: These are new students, and those are old students.

3. **أَيْنَ الْمُدَرِّسُونَ؟ هُمْ فِي غُرْفَةِ الْمُدِيرِ.**  
   *Ayna al-mudarrisoona? Hum fee ghurfati al-mudeeri.*  
   Urdu: اساتذہ کہاں ہیں؟ وہ پرنسپل کے کمرے میں ہیں۔  
   English: Where are the teachers? They are in the director's office.

4. **هٰذِهِ كُتُبٌ جَدِيدَةٌ.**  
   *Haadhihi kutubun jadeedatun.*  
   Urdu: یہ نئی کتابیں ہیں۔  
   English: These are new books.

5. **أَبْوَابُ الْمَسْجِدِ مَفْتُوحَةٌ.**  
   *Abwaabu al-masjidi maftoohatun.*  
   Urdu: مسجد کے دروازے کھلے ہیں۔  
   English: The doors of the mosque are open.

6. **لِمَنْ هٰذِهِ الْبُيُوتُ الصَّغِيرَةُ؟ هِيَ لِلْفَلَّاحِينَ.**  
   *Liman haadhihi al-buyootu as-sagheeratu? Hiya lil-fallaaheena.*  
   Urdu: یہ چھوٹے مکانات کس کے ہیں؟ وہ کسانوں کے ہیں۔  
   English: Whose are these small houses? They belong to the farmers.

7. **هٰذِهِ السَّيَّارَاتُ سَرِيعَةٌ جِدّاً.**  
   *Haadhihi as-sayyaaraatu saree'atun jiddan.*  
   Urdu: یہ گاڑیاں بہت تیز رفتار ہیں۔  
   English: These cars are very fast.

8. **أَلَكَ أَقْلَامٌ يَا عَلِيُّ؟ نَعَمْ، لِي أَقْلَامٌ كَثِيرَةٌ.**  
   *A-laka aqlaamun yaa 'Aliyyu? Na'am, lee aqlaamun katheeratun.*  
   Urdu: اے علی! کیا تمہارے پاس قلم ہیں؟ جی ہاں، میرے پاس بہت سے قلم ہیں۔  
   English: Do you have pens, O Ali? Yes, I have many pens.

9. **الْمَسَاجِدُ فِي مَدِينَتِنَا جَمِيلَةٌ.**  
   *Al-masaajidu fee madeenatinaa jameelatun.*  
   Urdu: ہمارے شہر میں مساجد خوبصورت ہیں۔  
   English: The mosques in our city are beautiful.

10. **هٰذَانِ كِتَابَانِ، وَهٰتَانِ سَاعَتَانِ.**  
    *Haadhaani kitaabaani, wa haataani saa'ataani.*  
    Urdu: یہ دو کتابیں ہیں، اور یہ دو گھڑیاں ہیں۔  
    English: These are two books, and these are two watches.

11. **خَرَجَ الطُّلَّابُ مِنَ الْفُصُولِ.**  
    *Kharaja at-tullaabu mina al-fusooli.*  
    Urdu: طلبہ کلاس رومز سے باہر نکلے۔  
    English: The students left the classrooms.

12. **أُولٰئِكَ النِّسَاءُ أُمَّهَاتُ الطَّالِبَاتِ.**  
    *Ulaa'ika an-nisaa'u ummahaatu at-taalibaati.*  
    Urdu: وہ عورتیں طالبات کی مائیں ہیں۔  
    English: Those women are the mothers of the female students.

## ⚠️ Part 5: Common Beginner Traps & Master Tutor Pro-Tips

### Trap 1: Using هٰؤُلَاءِ for inanimate objects
- **The Fatal Mistake**: Saying `هٰؤُلَاءِ كُتُبٌ` (*These are books*).
- **Master Rule**: **هٰؤُلَاءِ** is strictly reserved for human beings (people). For objects and animals, you must use **هٰذِهِ كُتُبٌ**.

### Trap 2: Using Plural Adjectives for Broken Plurals of Objects
- **Mistake**: Saying `الْكُتُبُ جُدُدٌ` (*The books are new*).
- **Master Rule**: Non-human plurals require a **Singular Feminine** adjective: **الْكُتُبُ جَدِيدَةٌ**.

### Trap 3: The Tanween Prohibition on مَسَاجِدُ
- **Diptote Rule**: Broken plurals on the pattern of *Mafaail* (like مَسَاجِدُ, مَفَاتِيحُ, مَكَاتِبُ) are diptotes (*Mamnoo' min as-Sarf*). They NEVER take Tanween; they end with a single Dammah: **مَسَاجِدُ**, never `مَسَاجِدٌ`.
