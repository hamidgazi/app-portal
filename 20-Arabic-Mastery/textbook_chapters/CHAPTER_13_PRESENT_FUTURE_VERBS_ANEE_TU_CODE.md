# 📖 باب سیزدہم: الفعل المضارع وشفرة أَنَيْتُ (فعل مضارع اور مستقبل کی چابی)
# Chapter 13: Present & Future Verbs — The ANEE-TU Prefix Code (Al-Fi'l al-Mudaari')

> **Master Tutor Law #13 (The Anee-tu Secret)**: 
> *Every single Present and Future verb in the Arabic language begins with one of the four letters in the mnemonic word **أَنَيْتُ** (**أ - ن - ي - ت**). If a verb does not start with one of these four keys, it is NOT a present tense verb!*

---

## 👨‍🏫 Part 1: Tutor Narrative & Conceptual Awakening

In Chapters 11 and 12, you conquered actions that already happened (**الفعل الماضي** — The Past Tense): *He went, I wrote, we heard*.

Now, how do you talk about what is happening **right now** (Present / حال) or what will happen **tomorrow** (Future / مستقبل)?

In Arabic, both the Present and Future are unified inside a single, dynamic verb tense known as:
$$\mathbf{الْفِعْلُ الْمُضَارِعُ} \quad (\textit{Al-Fi'l al-Mudaari'})$$

### The Meaning of "Mudaari'":
The word *Mudaari'* literally means "that which resembles". This verb is so flexible that it resembles a noun:
Unlike the past tense which was rigidly locked onto Fathah, the present tense flexes its vowels! Its default resting ending is a proud **Dammah / Pesh (`ُ`)**:
- Past: **ذَهَبَ** (*Dhahab-a* — ended in Fathah)
- Present: **يَذْهَبُ** (*Yadhhab-u* — ends in Dammah!)

---

## 🔑 The Four Golden Keys: أَنَيْتُ (A - Nee - Tu)

To transform any past tense root into the present tense, you must attach one of the **4 Mudara'ah Prefixes** to the front:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{أ} \quad \mathbf{ن} \quad \mathbf{ي} \quad \mathbf{ت}}$$

| Prefix Letter | Person & Role | Formula with ذ-ه-ب | Present Verb | English Meaning | Urdu Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **أَ** | **1st Person (أَنَا / I)** | أَ + ذْهَبُ | **أَذْهَبُ** (*Adhhabu*) | I go / I am going | میں جاتا ہوں |
| **نَ** | **1st Person Plural (نَحْنُ / We)** | نَ + ذْهَبُ | **نَذْهَبُ** (*Nadhhabu*) | We go / We are going | ہم جاتے ہیں |
| **يَ** | **3rd Person Masc. (هُوَ / He)** | يَ + ذْهَبُ | **يَذْهَبُ** (*Yadhhabu*) | He goes / He is going | وہ جاتا ہے |
| **تَ** | **2nd Masc. (أَنْتَ / You) OR 3rd Fem. (هِيَ / She)** | تَ + ذْهَبُ | **تَذْهَبُ** (*Tadhhabu*) | You go / She goes | تم جاتے ہو / وہ جاتی ہے |

Look how intuitive this is:
- **أَ** reflects **أَ**نَا (*I*)
- **نَ** reflects **نَ**حْنُ (*We*)
- **يَ** indicates **He** (*الغائب*)
- **تَ** indicates **You** (*المخاطب*) or **She** (*المؤنث*)

---

## 🚀 The Future Tense Engine: سَـ (*Sa-*) & سَوْفَ (*Sawfa*)

How do you turn "He goes" into **"He will go"**?  
Arabic makes this delightfully simple:
1. **Near Future (عنقریب — within hours or days)**: Glue the prefix **سَـ** (*Sa-*) directly to the front:
   $$\text{سَـ} + \text{يَذْهَبُ} = \mathbf{\text{سَيَذْهَبُ}} \quad (\textit{Sayadhhabu} — \text{He will go soon / وہ عنقریب جائے گا})$$
2. **Distant Future (دور کا مستقبل)**: Place the word **سَوْفَ** (*Sawfa*) before the verb:
   $$\text{سَوْفَ} + \text{يَعْلَمُونَ} = \mathbf{\text{سَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ}} \quad (\textit{Sawfa ya'lamoona} — \text{They will come to know / وہ جان لیں گے})$$

## 📐 Part 3: Visual Grammar Formulas & Blueprints (قواعد)

### Blueprint 1: The Present Tense Architectural Mold
$$\boxed{\begin{matrix} \text{Prefix (أ / ن / ي / ت)} & + & \text{1st Root (with Sukoon ْ)} & + & \text{2nd Root} & + & \text{3rd Root (with Dammah ُ)} \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow & & \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ \mathbf{يَ} & + & \mathbf{ذْ} & + & \mathbf{هَ} & + & \mathbf{بُ} \end{matrix} \implies \mathbf{\text{يَذْهَبُ}}}$$

### Blueprint 2: Pluralizing Present Tense Verbs
To make the 3rd person masculine plural ("They go"), add **ـُونَ** (*-oona*) to the end:
$$\boxed{\text{يَذْهَبُ} \quad (\text{He goes}) \qquad \longrightarrow \qquad \mathbf{\text{يَذْهَبُونَ}} \quad (\text{They go})}$$
$$\boxed{\text{تَذْهَبُ} \quad (\text{You go}) \qquad \longrightarrow \qquad \mathbf{\text{تَذْهَبُونَ}} \quad (\text{You all go})}$$

### Blueprint 3: Negating Present Tense Verbs
While past tense was negated with **مَا**, the present tense is negated in classical Arabic using **لَا** (*Laa*):
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{لَا}} + \text{يَشْرَبُ} \implies \mathbf{\text{لَا يَشْرَبُ}} \quad (\text{He does not drink / وہ نہیں پیتا})}$$
$$\boxed{\mathbf{\text{لَا}} + \text{أَعْلَمُ} \implies \mathbf{\text{لَا أَعْلَمُ}} \quad (\text{I do not know / مجھے معلوم نہیں})}$$

## 💬 Part 4: Reading Sentences & Dialogue Studio (قراءة ومحادثة)

Read these 12 sentences, identifying each prefix and vowel ending:

1. **إِلَى أَيْنَ تَذْهَبُ يَا أَخِي؟ أَذْهَبُ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ.**  
   *Ilaa ayna tadhhabu yaa akhee? Adhhabu ilaa al-masjidi.*  
   Urdu: اے میرے بھائی! آپ کہاں جا رہے ہیں؟ میں مسجد جا رہا ہوں۔  
   English: Where are you going, my brother? I am going to the mosque.

2. **مَاذَا تَفْعَلُ الْآنَ؟ أَنَا أَكْتُبُ رِسَالَةً إِلَى أَبِي.**  
   *Maadhaa taf'alu al-aana? Anaa aktubu risaalatan ilaa abee.*  
   Urdu: آپ اس وقت کیا کر رہے ہیں؟ میں اپنے والد کو خط لکھ رہا ہوں۔  
   English: What are you doing now? I am writing a letter to my father.

3. **يَشْرَبُ الطِّفْلُ الْحَلِيبَ كُلَّ صَبَاحٍ.**  
   *Yashrabu at-tiflu al-haleeba kulla sabaahin.*  
   Urdu: بچہ ہر صبح دودھ پیتا ہے۔  
   English: The child drinks milk every morning.

4. **أَتَعْرِفُ اللُّغَةَ الْعَرَبِيَّةَ؟ نَعَمْ، أَعْرِفُهَا قَلِيلاً.**  
   *A-ta'rifu al-lughata al-'Arabiyyata? Na'am, a'rifuhaa qaleelan.*  
   Urdu: کیا آپ عربی زبان جانتے ہیں؟ جی ہاں، میں اسے تھوڑا بہت جانتا ہوں۔  
   English: Do you know the Arabic language? Yes, I know it a little.

5. **أَيْنَ يَسْكُنُ مُدَرِّسُكَ؟ هُوَ يَسْكُنُ قَرِيباً مِنَ الْجَامِعَةِ.**  
   *Ayna yaskunu mudarrisuka? Huwa yaskunu qareeban mina al-jaami'ati.*  
   Urdu: آپ کے استاد کہاں رہتے ہیں؟ وہ یونیورسٹی کے قریب رہتے ہیں۔  
   English: Where does your teacher live? He lives near the university.

6. **نَحْنُ نَفْهَمُ الدَّرْسَ جَيِّداً، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلّٰهِ.**  
   *Nahnu nafhamu ad-darsa jayyidan, wal-hamdu lillaahi.*  
   Urdu: ہم سبق کو اچھی طرح سمجھتے ہیں، اور تمام تعریف اللہ کے لیے ہے۔  
   English: We understand the lesson well, and all praise is for Allah.

7. **مَتَى يَخْرُجُ الطُّلَّابُ مِنَ الْفُصُولِ؟ يَخْرُجُونَ فِي السَّاعَةِ الثَّانِيَةِ.**  
   *Mataa yakhruju at-tullaabu mina al-fusooli? Yakhrujoona fee as-saa'ati ath-thaaniyati.*  
   Urdu: طلبہ کلاسز سے کب نکلتے ہیں؟ وہ دو بجے نکلتے ہیں۔  
   English: When do the students leave the classrooms? They leave at two o'clock.

8. **أَمِي تَطْبُخُ الطَّعَامَ فِي الْمَطْبَخِ.**  
   *Ummee tatbukhu at-ta'aama fee al-matbakhi.*  
   Urdu: میری والدہ کچن میں کھانا پکا رہی ہیں۔  
   English: My mother is cooking food in the kitchen.

9. **هَلْ تَسْمَعُ الْأَذَانَ يَا بِلَالُ؟ نَعَمْ، أَسْمَعُهُ الْآنَ.**  
   *Hal tasma'u al-adhaana yaa Bilaalu? Na'am, asma'uhu al-aana.*  
   Urdu: اے بلال! کیا تم اذان سن رہے ہو؟ جی ہاں، میں اسے اس وقت سن رہا ہوں۔  
   English: Do you hear the Adhan, O Bilal? Yes, I hear it right now.

10. **سَأَذْهَبُ إِلَى مَكَّةَ فِي الْأُسْبُوعِ الْقَادِمِ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ.**  
    *Sa-adhhabu ilaa Makkata fee al-usboo'i al-qaadimi in shaa'Allaah.*  
    Urdu: میں انشاء اللہ اگلے ہفتے مکہ جاؤں گا۔  
    English: I will go to Makkah next week, God willing.

11. **سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ حَقِيقَةَ الْأَمْرِ.**  
    *Sawfa ta'lamoona haqeeqata al-amri.*  
    Urdu: عنقریب تم سب معاملے کی حقیقت جان لو گے۔  
    English: You all will come to know the reality of the matter.

12. **أَنَا لَا أَشْرَبُ الْقَهْوَةَ بِاللَّيْلِ.**  
    *Anaa laa ashrabu al-qahwata bil-layli.*  
    Urdu: میں رات کے وقت کافی نہیں پیتا ہوں۔  
    English: I do not drink coffee at night.

## ⚠️ Part 5: Common Beginner Traps & Master Tutor Pro-Tips

### Trap 1: Confusing تَذْهَبُ (You go) with تَذْهَبُ (She goes)
- **The Twin Forms**: Look closely at **تَذْهَبُ**. It can mean *"You [m] go"* OR *"She goes"*.
- **How to tell them apart**: Context decides!
  - If addressed to a man: **أَيْنَ تَذْهَبُ يَا أَخِي؟** $\to$ *Where are you going?*
  - If talking about a woman: **فَاطِمَةُ تَذْهَبُ إِلَى الْمَدْرَسَةِ** $\to$ *Fatimah is going to school.*

### Trap 2: Negating Present Tense with مَا
- **Mistake**: Saying `مَا أَذْهَبُ` for "I do not go".
- **Master Rule**: In standard Arabic, negate past tense with **مَا** (*Maa dhahabtu*) and present tense with **لَا** (*Laa adhhabu*).

### Trap 3: Putting Dammah on the Prefix Letter
- **Vowel Rule**: For basic 3-letter verbs, the prefix letter (أ - ن - ي - ت) ALWAYS carries a **Zabar (Fathah `َ`)**: **يَذْهَبُ**, **يَكْتُبُ**, **يَشْرَبُ**. Never say `يُذْهَبُ` (which turns into the passive voice!).

## 🔑 Complete Step-by-Step Answer Key

#### Drill 1 Answers:
1. أَنَا **أَ**كْتُبُ الرِّسَالَةَ. (Prefix is Alif with Fathah).
2. نَحْنُ **نَ**كْتُبُ الدَّرْسَ. (Prefix is Noon with Fathah).
3. هُوَ **يَ**كْتُبُ الْكِتَابَ. (Prefix is Yaa with Fathah).
4. أَنْتَ **تَ**كْتُبُ بِالْقَلَمِ. (Prefix is Taa with Fathah).
5. هِيَ **تَ**كْتُبُ الْوَاجِبَ. (Prefix is Taa with Fathah).

#### Drill 2 Answers:
1. **سَيَذْهَبُ حَامِدٌ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ.** (Hamid will soon go to the mosque).
2. **سَأَغْسِلُ سَيَّارَتِي.** (I will wash my car).
3. **سَنَقْرَأُ الْقُرْآنَ.** (We will read the Quran).

#### Drill 3 Answers:
1. **إِلَى أَيْنَ تَذْهَبُ؟ أَذْهَبُ إِلَى الْمَكْتَبَةِ.**
2. **الطُّلَّابُ يَقْرَءُونَ الْكِتَابَ.** (or يَقْرَأُ الطُّلَّابُ الْكِتَابَ).
